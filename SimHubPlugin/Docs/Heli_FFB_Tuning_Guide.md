# Helicopter FFB Tuning Guide (Plan 20 MSFS rotor cues)

A practical, fly-and-tune guide for the cyclic, collective and pedal cues
generated by `heli_unboosted_msfs.json` and the `_embedded/msfs_derivations.json`
sub-graph. Every value below is a default — they're starting points, not
prescriptions. The whole point of the relocation work was that you can
drag a slider mid-flight, so do that.

> **Convention.** All parameters appear as graph-editor sliders grouped by
> their `SignalGroup`:
> - **Aircraft.*** — physical/aero properties of the airframe
> - **Cyclic.*** — gain on the cyclic stick (pitch *and* roll share these)
> - **Collective.*** — gain on the collective lever
> - **FlightStickCollective.*** — collective base feel (damper/friction/load)

---

## TL;DR — tuning order

1. **Airframe basics** (Aircraft.RotorDiameterM, BladeCount, RotationSign,
   MaxTorqueNm). Get these right *before* you fly, from the manufacturer
   datasheet or sensible class defaults below.
2. **Cyclic feel in cruise** (Cyclic.LoadGain, Cyclic.SpringGain, KSpeedPitch,
   KSpeedRoll). The stick should resist forward pressure at 100 kt — pilot
   has to hold a forward push to counter the rotor's aft flapback force —
   and want to recentre when released.
3. **ETL bump** (Aircraft.EtlPeakKts/Width, Cyclic.EtlBumpGain,
   Cyclic.EtlBumpLoadGain/RollGain, Collective.EtlBumpGain). Accelerate
   from hover through ~25 kt; bump should be obvious but not violent.
4. **VRS hover-buffet** (Aircraft.VrsBuffetStart/Full, Cyclic/Collective.VrsBuffetGain,
   Cyclic.VrsAttenGain). Sustain hover + descent, cyclic should soften and
   the stick should buzz.
5. **VRS entry/exit hysteresis** (Aircraft.VrsTauUp/Down). Quickly enter and
   quickly exit VRS — exit should feel "sticky."
6. **RBS** (Aircraft.RbsThreshold/Width/MuCoupling, Cyclic.RbsLoadPitchGain/
   RollGain, Cyclic.RbsVib1Gain/VibNGain, Collective.RbsHeaveGain). At
   cruise + high collective + 1.5 G pull, the cyclic should fight back.
7. **Slap** (Aircraft.SlapOnsetKts/KSlap/SlapMax, Cyclic.slap_gain,
   Collective.slap_gain). Mostly cosmetic in normal cruise; only tune if it
   feels wrong on a fast aircraft.

Steps 3–6 each take ~5 minutes of flying once you've found a sane baseline.

## 1. Airframe basics

These are physical numbers. Get them right once per aircraft and stop
touching them.

| Param | What it is | Sensible range | How to find it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Aircraft.RotorDiameterM` | Main rotor disc diameter | 5–25 m | Datasheet (tip-to-tip) |
| `Aircraft.BladeCount` | Number of main rotor blades | 2–8 | Datasheet |
| `Aircraft.RotationSign` | +1 for CCW (US/MD500/AS350), -1 for CW (French) | +1 / -1 | Look at the rotor from above; CCW = +1 |
| `Aircraft.MaxTorqueNm` | Peak main-rotor torque | 100–5000 | Datasheet, or guess from MTOW |
| `Aircraft.TRBladeCount` | Tail rotor blade count | 2–6 | Datasheet |
| `Aircraft.TRGearRatio` | Tail rotor : main rotor RPM ratio | 1–10 | Datasheet |

> **Tip speed is computed**, not configured. The subgraph multiplies
> `Aircraft.RotorDiameterM × MSFS.MainRotor.Speed × π·1.944/60` each frame
> to get current `Vtip` in knots. This means tip speed correctly drops with
> rotor RPM in autorotation or droop, which then makes advance ratio `μ`
> climb at the same TAS — RBS becomes more likely when the rotor is
> under-speeding, which is physically correct.

### Aircraft-class starting points

| Class | Example | RotorDiameter | BladeCount | MaxTorque | RotationSign |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light 2-blade | R22 | 7.7 m | 2 | 150 Nm | +1 |
| Light 5-blade | MD 500E | 8.05 m | 5 | 800 Nm | +1 |
| Medium twin | H145 | 11.0 m | 4 (Fenestron) | 1100 Nm | +1 |
| Light French | AS350 | 10.7 m | 3 | 700 Nm | **-1** |
| Heavy | UH-60 | 16.4 m | 4 | 4000 Nm | +1 |

If you don't have a datasheet, set diameter to 8 m and torque to
`MTOW_kg × 0.8` Nm and tune from feel.

> **Airspeed convention.** All speed-sensitive cues (BladeAlph, ETL,
> Slap, VRS, RBS) use `MSFS.Speed.TAS` (true airspeed), not IAS. The
> rotor sees actual air velocity, not what your pitot tube reads. At
> sea level IAS ≈ TAS so this is invisible. At altitude, TAS is higher
> than IAS, so ETL/Slap/RBS will trigger at a lower indicated airspeed
> — which matches how real helicopters behave (ETL appears in IAS
> several knots earlier at altitude).
>
> **Reverse flight is supported.** TAS can go negative (real
> helicopters fly backward, especially during hover-taxi and
> precision repositioning). BladeAlph load sign flips correctly: in
> rearward flight the disc flap-tilts forward, so the cyclic load
> reverses and the pilot has to hold *aft* pressure. ETL/Slap stay
> dormant in reverse flight (their math gates them to forward
> velocity).

## 2. Cyclic in cruise (BladeAlph + LoadGain)

This is the dominant cyclic feel in forward flight. Dissymmetry of lift in
forward flight, combined with 90° rotor precession, tilts the disc **aft**
("flapback"). The rotor exerts an aft force on the cyclic — the pilot has to
**push forward** to hold it centered, holding that forward stick force
continuously while in cruise. The lateral analogue (sideslip-driven blade
asymmetry → lateral disc tilt) is modeled too but only fires when there's
actual sideslip.

| Param | What it changes | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Aircraft.KSpeedPitch` | How much aft-stick force grows with airspeed | 17 | 0–40 |
| `Aircraft.KSpeedRoll` | Lateral force grows with sideslip (beta) | 5 | 0–20 |
| `Cyclic.LoadGain` | Overall scale of BladeAlph → stick force | 0.2 | 0–5 |
| `Cyclic.SpringGain` | Centering spring | 0.45 | 0–5 |
| `Cyclic.DamperGain` | Velocity-proportional damping | varies | 0–5 |
| `Cyclic.Friction` | Static friction (sticktion) | varies | 0–5 |

### Tuning recipe

Climb out, accelerate to **80 kt straight and level**. The stick should
resist your forward input — i.e. let go and it should drift aft. You should
have to hold steady forward pressure to keep airspeed, scaling with how
fast you're going.

- Stick is dead in cruise → raise `Cyclic.LoadGain` (try 0.3–0.5)
- Aft force at cruise is so strong you can't push through it → lower
  `Aircraft.KSpeedPitch` (try 12)
- Stick doesn't want to lean into a 30° bank → raise `Cyclic.SpringGain`
- Stick oscillates around center → raise `Cyclic.DamperGain`

The BladeAlph load is multiplied by `rpm_norm` so it fades to zero with
rotor RPM — you'll feel it disappear in autorotation. That's correct.

## 3. ETL bump

Effective Translational Lift: the rotor transitions from recirculating its
own downwash to flying through clean air around 15–25 kt. A short vibration
+ forward nose-tuck + lateral roll (on CCW rotors, to the right).

| Param | What it changes | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Aircraft.EtlPeakKts` | Centre of the bump (in IAS) | 18 | 5–40 |
| `Aircraft.EtlWidthKts` | Width (1/e drop at ±this many kt) | 6 | 2–20 |
| `Cyclic.EtlBumpGain` | Pitch+Roll vibration amplitude at peak | 0.6 mm | 0–3 |
| `Collective.EtlBumpGain` | Collective vibration amplitude at peak | 0.4 mm | 0–3 |
| `Cyclic.EtlBumpLoadGain` | Aft load on cyclic at peak (pilot pushes forward) | 0.5 N | 0–3 |
| `Cyclic.EtlBumpLoadRollGain` | Lateral load on cyclic at peak (×RotationSign) | 0.4 N | 0–3 |

### Tuning recipe

Hover, then accelerate forward and **hold a steady ~20 kt for a few
seconds**. You should feel:

1. A short vibration centred around 18 kt (mostly on the collective)
2. A growing aft force on the cyclic — your forward-pressure hand has to
   work harder through the transition (this stacks with the BladeAlph
   flapback from §2)
3. On a CCW rotor (US), a lateral force you have to counter with **right**
   cyclic input — pilots are taught to anticipate this

**Tuning order:**
- Adjust `Aircraft.EtlPeakKts` so the bump centres at the speed it actually
  feels right (typically your aircraft's ETL is ~15–25 kt)
- Adjust `Aircraft.EtlWidthKts` for sharpness — smaller = sharper bump,
  bigger = smeared over a wider speed range
- Then tune the four gains independently for the right *intensity*

The roll component (`EtlBumpLoadRollGain`) is sign-flipped automatically by
`Aircraft.RotationSign` — set the gain positive and trust the sign.

## 4. VRS (Vortex Ring State)

VRS happens in hover-descent: the rotor falls into its own downwash, thrust
collapses, and the cyclic goes mushy with chaotic broadband buffet. Plan 20
models this as a **buffet force** (5–25 Hz band-limited noise injected on
the ESP32, not amplitude modulation of a clean tone) plus an **attenuation
of the BladeAlph cyclic stiffness**.

### 4a. Buffet character

| Param | What it changes | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Aircraft.VrsBuffetStart` | VRS level where buffet starts (0..1 effect space) | 0.2 | 0–1 |
| `Aircraft.VrsBuffetFull` | VRS level where buffet is fully developed | 0.8 | 0–1 |
| `Cyclic.VrsBuffetGain` | Buffet force amplitude on cyclic (both axes) | 1.5 N | 0–8 |
| `Collective.VrsBuffetGain` | Buffet force amplitude on collective | 1.0 N | 0–8 |

The "VRS effect" is `clamp((Rotor.VRS − 0.25) / 0.25, 0, 1)`, so 0 means
hover-no-descent and 1 means hover-with-1500-fpm-descent. Onset of
**buffet** kicks in once that effect exceeds `VrsBuffetStart`.

### 4b. Cyclic authority loss

| Param | What it changes | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Cyclic.VrsAttenGain` | How much VRS suppresses BladeAlph load | 0.5 | 0–1 |

`(1 − VrsAttenGain × vrs_effect)` is the multiplier on the existing
BladeAlph cyclic-stiffness term. At 1.0 with full VRS, the cyclic feels
completely dead. At 0.5 with full VRS, cyclic forces are halved. At 0,
VRS doesn't affect cyclic stiffness at all (only buffet).

### 4c. Entry/exit hysteresis

| Param | What it changes | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Aircraft.VrsTauUp` | Time constant when *entering* VRS | 0.25 s | 0.05–2 |
| `Aircraft.VrsTauDown` | Time constant when *leaving* VRS | 2.0 s | 0.5–10 |

VRS entry should feel snappy (TauUp small, ~0.25 s). VRS exit should feel
sticky — you have to fly out of it, not just push the cyclic forward and
have the feel instantly disappear (TauDown big, ~2 s).

### Tuning recipe

Hover at 500 ft AGL. **Reduce collective until you're descending at
1500+ fpm with zero forward speed.** You should feel:

1. The cyclic getting noticeably soft (BladeAlph attenuation)
2. A broadband, chaotic buffet on both cyclic and collective
3. When you push cyclic forward to escape, the buffet should *linger* for
   a couple of seconds even as you accelerate through 60 kt

**Adjust:**
- Buffet too quiet → raise `Cyclic.VrsBuffetGain` and `Collective.VrsBuffetGain`
- Buffet hammering the actuator → lower the gains
- Cyclic feels too "stiff" in VRS → raise `Cyclic.VrsAttenGain` (closer to 1.0)
- VRS sticks around forever after escape → lower `Aircraft.VrsTauDown`
- VRS doesn't kick in at all → lower `Aircraft.VrsBuffetStart`, or check
  that descent is steep enough (`Aircraft.DescentMaxFpm` defaults to 1500 —
  hitting saturation needs about that rate of sink)

**About the old N/rev VRS modulation:** `Cyclic.etl_gain` and
`Collective.etl_gain` in the vibration sub-graphs default to **0** now.
They previously multiplied VRS into the N/rev rotor harmonic, which the
literature says is the wrong character (real VRS is sub-rotor broadband,
not blade-passing). If you want to re-add that flavour on top of the buffet,
raise either to ~0.3 — but the buffet is the physically faithful path.

## 5. RBS (Retreating Blade Stall)

RBS is the limit-of-the-envelope cue: high airspeed + high collective +
manoeuvre load makes the retreating blade stall, producing pitch-up + roll
toward the retreating side + a 1/rev (or 2/rev on R22) cabin shake.

MSFS doesn't expose per-blade alpha, so this is a **synthetic envelope**:

```
demand = clamp(collective_pct/100,0,1) × max(G,1) × (1 + RbsMuCoupling × mu)
RBS    = clamp((demand − RbsThreshold) / RbsWidth, 0, 1)
```

where `mu = TAS / (RotorDiameterM × MainRotorRpm × π·1.944/60)`.

### Derivation params

| Param | What it changes | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Aircraft.RbsMuCoupling` | How much speed amplifies the demand | 2.5 | 0–10 |
| `Aircraft.RbsThreshold` | Demand level at which RBS begins | 1.6 | 0.5–5 |
| `Aircraft.RbsWidth` | Demand range over which RBS ramps 0→1 | 0.4 | 0.05–3 |

### Consumer gains

| Param | What it changes | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Cyclic.RbsVib1Gain` | 1/rev vibration at full RBS | 0.6 mm | 0–3 |
| `Cyclic.RbsVibNGain` | N/rev vibration (scales as RBS²) | 0.4 mm | 0–3 |
| `Collective.RbsHeaveGain` | Heave vibration on collective | 0.5 mm | 0–3 |
| `Cyclic.RbsLoadPitchGain` | Aft load on cyclic (pitch-up tendency; pilot pushes forward to counter) | 1.2 N | 0–5 |
| `Cyclic.RbsLoadRollGain` | Lateral load on cyclic toward retreating side (×RotationSign) | 0.8 N | 0–5 |

### Tuning recipe

Climb to 3000 ft AGL, accelerate to **110 kt at moderate collective**.
Then pull aft cyclic to ~1.5 G (60° bank or a sharp pull-up). You should
feel:

1. A 1/rev shake on the cyclic (or 2/rev if you're flying an R22)
2. Heave on the collective
3. Your forward-pressure budget shrinking — the cyclic wants to come further
   aft (pitch-up tendency). You need more forward effort to hold attitude;
   the correct recovery is reduce collective + ease the aft pull.
4. On a CCW rotor, lateral force toward the retreating side (left) — you
   have to hold right cyclic pressure to counter

**Adjust:**
- RBS fires in normal cruise (not just aggressive manoeuvres) → raise
  `Aircraft.RbsThreshold` (try 2.0)
- RBS never fires no matter how aggressive → lower `RbsThreshold` to 1.2,
  raise `RbsMuCoupling` to 4
- Onset feels too gradual / never gets to full intensity → lower `RbsWidth`
- Vibration too quiet / loud → adjust the three vibration gains in concert
  with the buffet gains so RBS is unmistakable but not louder than ETL

Recovery (reduce collective + slow down) should make RBS fade back to 0
within a second — there's no hysteresis on RBS.

## 6. Slap (BVI)

Blade-Vortex Interaction — the characteristic "wop-wop" of helicopters in
high-speed flight or steep descents. The plugin models this as an IAS-only
linear ramp.

| Param | What it changes | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Aircraft.SlapOnsetKts` | TAS at which slap starts | 20 | 0–80 |
| `Aircraft.KSlap` | Slap ramp gradient | 0.03 | 0–0.2 |
| `Aircraft.SlapMax` | Cap on slap envelope | 0.1 | 0–1 |
| `Cyclic.slap_gain` | Slap → cyclic N/rev vibration | 1.0 mm | 0–2.55 |
| `Collective.slap_gain` | Slap → collective N/rev vibration | 0.5 mm | 0–2.55 |

This is vibration-only — no cyclic load coupling. Per plan 20 §3.4.2 the
literature has no static slap load.

### Tuning recipe

Cruise at 100+ kt. There should be an audible (well, *felt*) low-amplitude
N/rev wop-wop. Mostly background texture. Tune only if you notice it's
absent or overwhelming.

## 7. Cyclic vibration baseline (rotor texture)

These are the always-on rotor harmonic textures — the cabin "running"
vibration of a helicopter in flight. Independent of any of the cues above.

| Param | What it changes | Default | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Cyclic.gain_1rev` | 1/rev amplitude scaling with BladeAlph | 0.5 mm/deg | 0–2.55 |
| `Cyclic.mass_imbalance` | 1/rev term from blade mass imbalance × rpm² | 0.05 mm | 0–2.55 |
| `Cyclic.track_drift` | 1/rev term from rotor tracking × rpm | 0.1 mm | 0–2.55 |
| `Cyclic.base_nrev` | Always-on N/rev amplitude × rpm_norm | 0.3 mm | 0–2.55 |
| `Cyclic.base_2nrev` | Always-on 2N/rev amplitude | 0.1 mm | 0–2.55 |

Equivalent params exist for collective with `Collective.*` prefix.

## Sanity-check matrix

A quick reference for *what should happen when*:

| Manoeuvre | Expected feel |
|---|---|
| Hover, no descent | Centering force, mild N/rev base texture, no buffet |
| Hover + 1500+ fpm descent | Cyclic softens, broadband buffet on cyclic + collective |
| Accel through 18 kt | Short bump on collective, forward+right push on cyclic |
| Cruise 100 kt | Forward stick force, mild slap, no buffet, no RBS |
| 1.5 G pull at cruise | Onset of 1/rev shake (or 2/rev on 2-blade), aft-cyclic resistance, lateral resistance to retreating side |
| Autorotation (low RPM) | All cyclic load fades because everything ×rpm_norm |

If any of these are missing, that's where to focus your tuning.

## Troubleshooting

**"Feels like there's no force at all"** — check `FlightStickPitch.LoadGain`
and `FlightStickPitch.SpringGain` are nonzero. Also check the ESP32
hardware-level damping / friction aren't muting everything downstream.

**"Stick fights me everywhere all the time"** — too much damping, friction,
or load gain. Halve everything until it's responsive again, then add back
one term at a time.

**"VRS never engages"** — check that `Rotor.VRS` is actually rising. In
the graph editor, the include preview shows the live value. If it's stuck
at 0.25 (the floor), your descent rate isn't high enough or
`Aircraft.DescentMaxFpm` is set too high.

**"ETL bump is in the wrong place"** — every aircraft is different. Don't
trust the 18 kt default; fly and observe. Some 2-blade light helis ETL
as low as 12 kt.

**"RBS fires constantly"** — `Aircraft.RbsThreshold` is too low for the
collective/G profile of this aircraft. Raise it until RBS only fires
during deliberate aggressive manoeuvres.

**"After-effect of VRS lingers way too long"** — `Aircraft.VrsTauDown`
is too high. Try 1.0 s. If the effect is symmetric (entry and exit feel
the same), check that `lag_asym` was actually picked up by the runtime —
the C# `GraphCompiledEvaluator` needs the `lag_asym` Func case (plan 20
phase D).

## Persistence

All these parameters live in `AircraftFfbProfile.GraphParamValues` keyed
by their full `Group.Suffix` name. They save with the profile and follow
the existing tiered config (Hardware → Profile → User override). No
schema migration needed — adding a new parameter just means it didn't
exist in the saved profile, and it'll fall back to the sub-graph's default.
